** Abstract**

This paper proposes a unified, evidence-based approach to understanding global events from the perspective of biblical eschatology in the United States context. It utilizes the prophetic Scriptures in Daniel, Matthew, Thessalonians, and Revelation, coupled with current global information, to discuss the relationship between technological innovation, economic instability, ecological disturbance, and geopolitical unease, in light of the trends expected in Scripture. Placed in the format of a systematic review and meta-analysis, the study will combine quantitative indicators, including AI proliferation, GDP instability, and conflict frequency, with theological interpretation to create a unified interpretive framework. The timeframe (2000–2025) was chosen so that the most significant period of modern transformation in the world can be captured. With the help of the PRISMA 2020 protocol, the research will identify and compare institutional sources such as the International Monetary Fund, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, and the Stanford AI Index with the eschatological themes. Through the combination of empirical study and hermeneutical theology, the study will create a model that connects measurable phenomena to prophetic archetypes, thereby bringing faith and reason together.

** 1.0 Introduction**

The escalation of global events and technologies has prompted humanity to seek answers beyond the available research and data, as it questions its destiny.[^1] This study will apply the lens of biblical eschatology to illuminate these developments, not with the intention of publishing predictions, but to promote understanding and critical analysis of these events as they unfold, including their potential effects on the existence and civilization of humanity. The study seeks to spark and inspire discussions between reason and revelation, Scripture and science, and faith and research. In a world that is currently more connected than at any other time, with reportedly among the highest levels of moral decadence, a critical analysis of the prophetic discourse in the Bible offers critique and hope, facilitating a humble observation of the unfolding of eschatology and aligning with the salvation offered by Jesus Christ.

*** 1.1. Purpose and Background**

The increased human craft and innovation, as manifested in contemporary civilization, including the emergence of Artificial Intelligence (AI), the rise of climate disasters, and the growing instability in state and geopolitics, triggers both anxiety and imagination about the destiny of humanity.[^2] The present American population is paradoxically challenged by the increasing technological advancements and their accompanying anxieties for humanity, as evidenced by the growing political divisions, economic precarity, and moral polarization.[^3] Therefore, this is the ideal moment to hermeneutically discuss biblical eschatology, not only in the interpretation of spiritual discourse but also in the analysis of societal evolution. Modern events potentially allude to the Biblical description of the end times, which is termed "acharit hayyamim," the Hebrew scriptures literally translating to "the latter days," which may not be translated as a catastrophic end but an age of the culmination of revelations, also known as the unveiling (apokalypsis) of divine systems within disruptions.[^4] This study will assess numerous global events, such as the acceleration of technologies, shifting economic trends, and ecological disruptions, from an eschatological perspective to examine the evidence of archetypal biblical prophecies in empirical data from research and practice, as manifested in the United States.

*** 1.2. Research Rationale***

Few methodologically rigorous studies interpret the Bible based on quantitative data from the analysis of global events. As prophesied by Ha-Navi Daniyel (The Prophet Daniel), "…the time of the end (acharit hayyamim), when many will rush here and there, and knowledge (da'at) will increase" (Dan 12:4 NLT), this research will take advantage of the numerous sources of data and information to prove the Bible's eschatology. The study seeks to design an interpretation model based on theological semiotics, integrated with quantifiable data on global events and developments in research articles, industry reports, and news data in the United States, to facilitate both a spiritual and scientific analysis of the discourse on global evolution.

*** 1.3. Research Problem***

How can a data-based, Biblically Inspired interpretation model facilitate American scholars and practitioners in the analysis of the relationship between scriptural prophecies and unfolding global events in a contemporary, sensitive, and accurate approach?

** 2.0 Statement of the Problem and Research Questions**

** 2.1. Central Problem**

Despite the prevalence of apocalyptic and eschatological topics in American media and theological discourses, an interpretative model has not been developed to relate biblical prophecies and global events in a quantitative context, leading to methodologically limited and fragmented readings that are largely sensational and narrative.

***2.2. Research Questions***

RQ1. What is the best approach in interpreting Biblical eschatology based on global events and their impacts as observed in the United States?

RQ2. How can quantifiable data, such as political crises, economic indices, climate metrics, and AI growth, be used to prove an eschatological apocalypse?

RQ3. What are the impacts of American media interpretation of "crises" and "end times" on the interpretation of scriptural prophecies?

RQ4. What are the potential implications of the interpretation model on responsible research and public discussions?

** 3.0 Aim and Objectives of the Study**

** 3.1. Aim of the Study**

To develop a detailed interpretive model based on quantifiable data for analyzing global trends from an eschatological perspective, drawing on research studies and media news from the United States.

*** 3.2. Objectives of the Study****

1. Identify major eschatological indicators in the Bible (New Living Version).
2. Conduct a systematic review and analysis of empirical studies between 2000 and 2025 on global events and their impacts in the United States.
3. Design a meta-analytically replicable model that conforms to global trends in Biblical scriptures.
4. Decode the eschatological themes in American mainstream media narratives and reports.
5. Suggest methodological approaches to the integration of scientific research and theological studies.

** 4.0 Review of Related Literature**

Contemporary theological reflections and discourses are increasingly exposed to data, both as its facilitator and its critic.[^5] International changes, such as in economies, climatic events, and technological advancements, can be effectively understood through interpretative frameworks that weave scriptures through available research, data, and events to facilitate the interpretation of their unfolding.[^6] In this context, biblical eschatology offers both the prophesied future events and the epistemology of their revelation (apokalypsis), integrating the Bible into the discourse of history.[^7] Amidst the global events unfolding, the American Bible and Prophetic Conference reports that cultural-theological studies in the United States are increasingly adopting integrative approaches to studying prophetic scriptures through the lens of quantifiable global data and measurable sociocultural events.[^8] The Bible, literature, and research shall significantly inform this study's background on theology, empirical studies on international events and technological advancement, and media narratives and publications, to design a model that is effectively conscious of spiritual discourse through the truth (emet) as provided by the Scriptures, and knowledge (da'at).

*** 4.1. The use of Biblical Scriptures***

The primary epistemic framework for this study will be the Bible, with the interpretation of the reviewed literature grounded in the Holy Scriptures. The study will be grounded in a hermeneutical basis, utilizing Scriptural quotes from the eschatological discourses throughout the Bible, with a slightly higher focus on the books of Daniel, Matthew, Thessalonians, and Revelation, from which global events and empirical data will be synthesized. The Scripture shall be thoroughly researched, including its lexical denotation in relevant instances where etymological explanations of its Hebrew, Amharic, Greek, and Latin would facilitate further understanding.

** 4.2. Theological Foundations**

The Christian and Hebrew interpretations of biblical eschatology are not annihilistic, but instead cyclically revolving with rhythms of realization and change. Hebrew's "acharit hayyamim" translates to "the latter days," which initiates not an end but a transition to other phases of life, highlighting potential changes in human-known terrestrial and celestial life forms.[^9] The foundation of eschatology in theology is laid by Calvin and Augustine, who interpret it as a call for morality rather than the end of life.[^10] In modern theology, the concept has been approached as "hopeful eschatology," projecting its crises as transformative. In the Hebrew text, apocalypse is reframed as Tikvah (hope), indicating the promise of the emergence of divine life forms within human chaos.

***# 4.3. Media and Cultural Studies***

The American media culture is always projecting the instability of the world into the apocalyptic idioms, i.e., the economic meltdown, AI singularity, and climate Armageddon. Eschatological imagery is an organizational framework for national consciousness and policy discourse, as recorded by researchers such as Paul Boyer, Peter Horsfield, and Stewart Hoover.[^11] The phonetic similarity between the biblical word "yom YHWH" (day of the Lord) and the media discourse of doomsday highlights the continued existence of theological archetypes in the secular narrative.[^12] These analyses demonstrate that more often than not, popular communication tends to secularize sacred images, turning prophecy into spectacle yet retaining the moral urgency that defines Scriptural discourses.

*** 4.4. Empirical Data and Eschatological Correlation***

The IMF (2025), IPCC (2023), and Stanford HAI (2025) reports are all quantitative sources that document an objective increase in change in technological, climatic, and geopolitical spheres. Reading between the lines of Daniel 12:4, where da'at (knowledge) is promised to keep growing, these patterns are scriptural paradigms of acceleration before transformation. The spread of information and movement, so many will go to and fro, is what the ancients had imagined to be ominous omens of change. Empirical evidence is, therefore, a solid basis for exploring whether quantifiable phenomena are representative of eschatological typologies that have been outlined in the biblical canon.

*** 4.5. Gap in Research***

Despite the rich theological and empirical research, there is currently no unifying framework among biblical hermeneutics, quantitative analysis, and U.S. media discourse.[^13] The existing methods are instead divided between taking a spiritually interpretive approach, which lacks empirical evidence, and utilizing a data-driven methodology without considering the theological nuances.[^14] This study fills that gap by postulating an evidence-based, systematic interpretive framework that will inform future academic work, theological books, and analysis in the public media. The paper aims to contribute to the development of eschatological research by integrating spiritual intuition and scientific explanation through an integrative synthesis of da'at, emet, and Tikvah.

** 5.0 Theoretical and Conceptual Framework**

*** 5.1. Biblical–Theological Framework***

This paper will be based on biblical eschatology, which discusses the theological inquiry of the latter days, or eschatology, as found in Daniel 2, 7; Matthew 24; 1 Thessalonians 4, 15-18: 5, 1-11; and Revelation 13. These Scriptures explain Godly order in the storm of history. They will be interpreted using hermeneutic phenomenology, aimed at identifying divine revelation available in human events, and the understanding of prophecy not as a deterministic, predictive statement, but as a participatory revelation (apokalypsis), in which God's plan is revealed through the work of human beings.

***5.2. Analytical Framework***

Applying systems theory, the world is found to be interconnected in a prophetic interdependency established in Scripture, where economic, political, and moral changes form a single eschatological system.[^15] Meta-analytic modelling will synthesize both empirical indicators, which will be quantitative, such as economic turmoil, the spread of artificial intelligence, the rate of conflict, and climate change, as well as the biblical accounts of historical fulfilment. Through integrative theology, the paper establishes a connection between the sovereignty of God (malkhut Elohim) and human agency (adam ha'oseh), which then prefigures moral responsibility as the key factor in understanding the continuation of the prophetic tradition that has been characteristic of the current world upheaval.

** 6.0 Methodology

*** 6.1. Research Design***

The current study will involve a Systematic Review and meta-analysis in a theological-empirical context, thus incorporating quantitative measures alongside qualitative theological exegesis. The time frame of the review spans 2000 to 2025; thus, a period marked by fast-paced technological, economic, and geopolitical transformation. The study will utilize a bifurcated approach by (1) conducting quantitative studies of worldwide indicators to clarify any measurable occurrences in the fields of technology, economy, conflict, and environmental effects, and (2) conducting qualitative exegesis of eschatological Scripture to determine any conceptual similarities between the phenomena of the empirical world and biblical oracles. This integrative method allows for the development of an interpretive model that relates da'at (knowledge) and emet (truth), encompassing both empirical and revelatory levels of cognition.

*** 6.2. Population and Sampling***

The sources will include the biblical text (New Living Translation) and peer-reviewed articles on theological, technological, and economic matters, along with secondary information in the form of institutional data from the International Monetary Fund, the World Bank, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, the AI Index, and Reuters. The inclusion criteria will focus on works that provide empirical measures of the global patterns related to the motifs of eschatology, including war, deception, transformation, control, and environmental upheaval. Sampling will be selective in terms of scholarship and institutional authority.

*** 6.3. Data Collection and Extraction***

The PRISMA 2020 protocol of systematic reviews will be followed in data collection. The percentage of AI investments, GDP volatility, frequency of cyber incidents, and global conflict indices will be taken as quantitative variables. Eschatological verses will be thematically coded, i.e., milchamah [war], sheker [deception], shilton [control], and tikkun [renewal], to enable comparison to empirical categories.

*** 6.4. Data Analysis***

The statistical analyses (meta-analysis) will be used to calculate weighted means and correlation coefficients among the relevant variables. The resulting cross-tabulation will be of the result with systematically coded eschatological categories. A thematic hermeneutic analysis will then be employed to discern the theological implications in these correlations and identify possible echoes with the prophetic tradition.

*** 6.5. Reliability and Validity***

Theological, economic, environmental, and technological data triangulation will improve the reliability of the methodology. A review by experts, including theologians, data scientists, and ethicists, will be conducted to legitimize the interpretations, thereby ensuring academic rigor and spiritual sensitivity.
